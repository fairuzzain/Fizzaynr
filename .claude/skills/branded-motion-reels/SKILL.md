## Hooks, stories and useful short-form content

Use for new scripts, hook alternatives, captions and content audits. Preserve approved wording when the user asks only for motion changes. These are writing and testing heuristics, not a formula for virality.

### Establish what the viewer will get
Use existing context to identify the viewer, their current task or frustration, the desired action, available evidence and runtime. Ask only for missing information that changes the story. Choose one practical outcome per short Reel. A broad personal brand can address a particular problem without claiming an industry specialty.

Choose an angle from real material: a working demonstration, an unexpected constraint, a mistake and its repair, a decision between alternatives, or a repeatable method. Verify tool names, availability, pricing and feature claims when they matter. Do not invent personal experience, tests, savings, results or scarcity to fill an arc. Treat trend listings and competitor views as leads to investigate, not evidence that our version will perform.

### Develop the opening as a package
For a new piece, draft a small set of genuinely different angles, usually 3–5. For each, pair the spoken opener with the first visual and a short readable headline. The viewer should understand the subject with sound off; the voice and image can add different information without competing. Captions may repeat speech for accessibility. Avoid a separate logo intro that postpones the subject.

| Angle | What makes it credible | Original example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Show the result | The actual artifact appears immediately | “This animation started as one sentence.” |
| Expose a friction point | The obstacle is recognizable and demonstrated | “Your AI Reel keeps looking like slides. Start with the story.” |
| Reveal a mechanism | Show the step responsible for the outcome | “I put the motion workflow in a Skill.md file.” |
| Test a limit | State the test and show what happens | “Can one brief become a finished 15-second Reel?” |
| Challenge an assumption | Give evidence for the narrower claim | “A longer prompt won’t fix a disconnected script.” |

These examples require the matching evidence. Do not recycle them for unrelated topics. An approved provocative hook can keep its energy; make the body promptly show the actual scope of the claim. Do not invent layoffs, industry replacement, exclusivity or performance numbers.

Judge candidates on subject clarity, viewer relevance, specificity, curiosity and the strength of the available payoff. Reject vague suspense, delayed topics, confusing phrasing and hooks that promise more than the video demonstrates. A score is editorial judgment, not a predicted view count. Select the strongest supported promise, not simply the loudest sentence.

### Build a story that survives without the motion
Write the payoff first. Outline the smallest set of beats that earns it. Use a causal test: does this happen because of the previous beat, solve its obstacle, or reveal its consequence? “But” and “so” can help diagnose the outline; do not force them into every sentence or manufacture conflict in a straightforward tutorial.

Keep one subject, example and vocabulary throughout. If the opener shows a Reel, follow that same Reel through its brief, creation, refinement and export. Do not jump from a tool announcement to an unrelated skill pitch and then an unrelated result. Every line should advance the promise. A useful demonstration is the payoff; an offer to send a file should not replace all substance.

Flexible 15-second starting shape:

| Approximate time | Story job | Visual job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 s | Name the specific promise or problem | Show the result or the friction immediately |
| 3–7 s | Reveal the mechanism or meaningful change | Show the tool, file or action that explains it |
| 7–11.5 s | Demonstrate the consequence or usable step | Continue the same example; show evidence |
| 11.5–15 s | Resolve the promise and give a next step | Hold a clear ending with time to read |

Adapt this to the material. Roughly 30–40 spoken words may fit 15 seconds, depending on delivery and pauses. Record or synthesize the voice and measure it. Shorten the script before speeding up speech. Leave breathing room around the reveal and ending.

Read the spoken text by itself. If it still sounds like disconnected captions, fix the causal sequence before animating. Vary sentence length, keep concrete verbs and write as if explaining to one interested person. Make the ending answer the opening; a natural callback can help, but a forced loop can weaken clarity.

### Make the visual serve each sentence
Use a compact beat sheet: time, voiceover, headline, visual evidence/action, transition reason. Animate the action the sentence describes. Use camera travel to move between connected ideas, not to hide missing content. Introduce one main reading task at a time and check the actual small-screen layout. A result, cursor action or changed state usually explains more than unrelated decorative imagery.

### Caption and CTA
Give the caption one useful addition: a setup step, limitation, example or context that did not fit the voiceover. Keep the first line specific. Use tags and emojis selectively to match the user's brand.

Choose a next step that serves the viewer and the content goal. Offer a file only if it exists and contains the promised value. Confirm its accessible link and delivery route. Say whether requests are manual when automation is inactive. A follow condition must actually be checked before presenting it as enforced. Do not say “sent” until delivery succeeds. Avoid unrelated tag requests, artificial controversy and empty comments requested only to inflate engagement.

### Edit, then learn from results
Use focused passes: comprehension, relevance, concrete detail, evidence, natural voice and timing. Remove generic hype and repeated claims; preserve useful technical terms. Challenge the draft from a new viewer's perspective: what is this, why would I care, what did I learn, and did the ending deliver? Stop revising when a specific weakness has been resolved rather than running endless self-scoring loops.

Save the chosen hook, angle, promise, script, caption, evidence and one test hypothesis with the production notes. Where analytics expose them, compare early retention, average watch time relative to runtime, completion, shares/saves per reach and meaningful actions such as profile visits or resource requests. Record unavailable fields rather than inferring them. Change one main factor in a focused test. A few likes or one comment cannot establish that a hook is a winner.

## Design and motion

### Read the motion reference, not just its cover
Watch transitions and camera motion in the user's references. Record pacing, depth, type, image treatment and sound separately. Stills cannot establish an animation style. Use reference media for analysis, not as output footage without appropriate rights.

References reviewed for the accepted October 2026 Alascale Reel:
- https://www.pinterest.com/pin/5207355816250053/ — Velvele, Motion Templates / After Effects: quick collage assembly, image grids, bold typography and scale shifts.
- https://www.pinterest.com/pin/70437491746272/ — Introducing Finqube: gradient depth, floating UI, perspective, cursor interactions and camera travel synchronized to narration.

### Camera-led composition
The rejected version looked like slides: isolated scenes with tiny scale changes. The accepted revision places five content groups in one continuous world and moves a shared camera among them. Pull back to reveal the route, travel decisively, then arrive at a readable close view. Keep title/body roles stable. Leave a final CTA hold; avoid continuous motion that prevents reading.

The bundled example is 1080 × 1920, 30 fps, 450 frames. Panel centers: (0,0), (1600,-300), (1600,1700), (3400,1700), (3400,-300). Camera transitions around frames 68–100, 144–174, 268–294 and 338–368. Zoom pulls back near .52 and returns near 1. These are working example timings, not universal values. Use one camera transform with a fixed origin. Apply small floating drift only to independent collage layers.

### Type, icons and hierarchy
Use a strong readable sans and a contrasting readable serif for selected important words. The user rejected thin Bodoni italic at video size; Manrope plus Georgia italic was accepted. Check the actual serif's availability on the render host; Georgia is a system font and is not redistributed — choose a licensed bundled replacement on systems lacking it.

Explicitly box each headline line; measure at the largest camera scale. Avoid accidental wrapping and collisions between title lines. Shorten copy before shrinking it into unreadability. Check the 9:16 crop during travel as well as at rests. The example uses fixed-width 790 px headline lines with nowrap, staggered by 6 frames, translating 24 px into place. Serif emphasis gently pops into its settled scale.

Animate icons as meaningful actions: draw arrow strokes, pop and settle, cursor presses with button response, a spinner becoming a check, and progress synchronized to the action. Decorative motion should not compete with the spoken message. All animation must derive from Remotion frame/time, not wall-clock time, CSS transitions, Math.random or stateful playback.

### Connector repair
The user liked the camera-led result but found lines crossing text and missing their end anchors. Routes now live behind content, outside panel bounds, with explicit edge anchor circles. Keep panel centers fixed; floating the parent moves it away from a fixed path. Use SVG pathLength=1, dasharray=1 and clamped progress for reliable endpoints. Inspect routes during travel and at the last frame. A line should help spatial continuity without passing through copy or images.

### Generated images
Create original image assets separately and integrate them into the camera world. Specify material, composition, light, negative space and brand palette, without asking the generator for critical text. The accepted sculpture prompt used mirror-polished chrome intertwined with cobalt satin, warm ivory paper, studio key light and soft shadows, asymmetric composition, no text or people. It complemented generated architectural images. Avoid using image generation to repair a rendering bug.

## Audio and export

### Voice first
Keep a 15-second script short enough to speak naturally. Place each phrase in a timed slot, then choreograph the camera around it. The accepted example used six phrases at .20, 2.90, 5.30, 8.20, 9.60 and 11.55 seconds. Voiceover is part of the deliverable when the user requested it.

Local Kokoro ONNX is a working free stock-voice solution. Runtime: https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx (MIT); model v1.0 Apache 2.0. Download models only when needed, from that project's official model-files-v1.0 release. The example uses am_michael, speed 1.08, en-us; a stock synthetic voice, not a voice clone. An anonymous edge-tts endpoint returned HTTP 403 once, so it was replaced rather than retried indefinitely.

`generate_voiceover.py` (appendix) accepts a JSON array of {text,start,slot}, model/voice paths, and an output path. It normalizes speech to -16 LUFS / -1.5 dBTP, fits only overlong phrases, rejects overlap and excessive speedup, and fills the requested duration with silence. Dependencies: kokoro-onnx, soundfile, numpy, ffmpeg. Use an isolated environment. Shorten an overfull script instead of forcing unnatural speech.

The template contains a sample stock voiceover, an original synthesized music bed and whoosh/click/ding assets from https://remotion.media — replace them when the story changes. Set narration clearly above music/effects and listen to the exported mix. Do not silently change the user's language or claim synthetic narration is their recorded voice.

### Reliable assets and rendering
Keep media in public/ and resolve it with staticFile. Check files and HTTP responses if preview says an image cannot load. Wait for fonts and image decoding using supported Remotion components/loaders. A reachable Studio page is not proof the final media works. Derive paths from the actual project; do not assume localhost:3000 or a previous server remains active.

Pinned accepted template: Remotion 4.0.534 (@remotion/cli, @remotion/fonts, @remotion/media), React 19.2.3, TypeScript 5.9.3. Run the project's TypeScript check (`tsc --noEmit`) after code edits. Keep all Remotion packages at the same version. The template's remotion.config.ts sets Rspack, PNG video image format, swangle GL, concurrency 1 and overwrite output.

### Flicker incident and diagnosis
The first accepted-looking export used installed Chrome with --gl=angle, JPEG frames and concurrency 2. A small contact sheet missed intermittent corruption. Full sequential analysis found isolated luma drops, repeated tiles replacing the frame, and partly drawn image layers — exported-frame corruption, not authored camera motion. When that signature appears, test the same short range with --gl=swangle, PNG frame capture and concurrency 1 before rerendering the full piece. In that incident the replacement was clean across 450 reviewed frames (zero isolated luma spikes vs eleven). Verify each new export rather than assuming flags always fix it.

```
npx remotion render MotionReel /absolute/output/fixed-reel.mp4 --codec=h264 --crf=17 --pixel-format=yuv420p --concurrency=1 --image-format=png --gl=swangle
```
Use --browser-executable only if needed on the current host. Export to a fresh filename so an older video is not mistaken for the fix.

Screen the entire decoded video with check_export.py, inspect flagged frames and their neighbors, check frame count/dimensions/duration with ffprobe, and view transitions at normal speed. A few stills are insufficient. Luma checks can miss corruption that preserves mean brightness. If software rendering still drops only canvas images, try switching CanvasImage to the standard Remotion Img component and retest. Avoid stacking unverified workarounds.

## Appendix: scripts (write to a scripts/ folder in the project when needed)

### check_export.py
```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Screen every decoded frame; flags require visual review, not automatic rejection."""
import argparse,json,subprocess
from pathlib import Path

def run(args):
    return subprocess.run(args,check=True,stdout=subprocess.PIPE,stderr=subprocess.PIPE).stdout

def main():
    a=argparse.ArgumentParser(description=__doc__)
    a.add_argument('video',type=Path)
    a.add_argument('--output',type=Path,required=True)
    a.add_argument('--sheets',action='store_true',help='Create sequential 50-frame contact sheets')
    args=a.parse_args();args.output.mkdir(parents=True,exist_ok=True)
    probe=json.loads(run(['ffprobe','-v','error','-show_streams','-show_format','-of','json',str(args.video)]))
    raw=run(['ffmpeg','-v','error','-i',str(args.video),'-vf','scale=108:192,format=gray','-an','-f','rawvideo','-pix_fmt','gray','-'])
    size=108*192
    if len(raw)%size: raise RuntimeError('Incomplete decoded frame')
    means=[sum(raw[i:i+size])/size for i in range(0,len(raw),size)]
    steps=[i for i in range(1,len(means)) if abs(means[i]-means[i-1])>8]
    spikes=[i for i in range(1,len(means)-1) if abs(means[i]-(means[i-1]+means[i+1])/2)>5 and abs(means[i-1]-means[i+1])<3]
    report={'video':str(args.video.resolve()),'decoded_frames':len(means),'streams':probe['streams'],'format':probe['format'],'mean_luma':means,'large_step_frames':steps,'isolated_spike_frames':spikes,'interpretation':'Review flagged frames and neighbors. Legitimate cuts can trigger flags; corruption can escape luma checks.'}
    (args.output/'export-report.json').write_text(json.dumps(report,indent=2)+'\n')
    if args.sheets:
        for start in range(0,len(means),50):
            end=min(start+49,len(means)-1)
            vf=f"select='between(n,{start},{end})',scale=180:320,tile=10x5:padding=2:margin=2:color=gray"
            run(['ffmpeg','-y','-v','error','-i',str(args.video),'-vf',vf,'-frames:v','1',str(args.output/f'frames-{start:03d}-{end:03d}.jpg')])
    print(json.dumps({'decoded_frames':len(means),'large_step_frames':steps,'isolated_spike_frames':spikes,'report':str(args.output/'export-report.json')}))
if __name__=='__main__':main()
```

### generate_voiceover.py
```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Generate a timed stock-voice track with local Kokoro and ffmpeg."""
import argparse,json,subprocess,tempfile
from pathlib import Path
import numpy as np
import soundfile as sf
from kokoro_onnx import Kokoro

def main():
    ap=argparse.ArgumentParser(description=__doc__)
    for name in ['model','voices','plan','output']:ap.add_argument('--'+name,type=Path,required=True)
    ap.add_argument('--duration',type=float,required=True)
    ap.add_argument('--voice',default='am_michael');ap.add_argument('--lang',default='en-us');ap.add_argument('--speed',type=float,default=1.08)
    args=ap.parse_args();plan=json.loads(args.plan.read_text());sr=24000
    if args.duration<=0:ap.error('duration must be positive')
    slots=sorted(plan,key=lambda p:p['start'])
    previous_end=0
    for p in slots:
        if not p['text'].strip() or p['start']<previous_end or p['slot']<=0 or p['start']+p['slot']>args.duration:ap.error('Plan has empty text, overlapping slots or times outside duration')
        previous_end=p['start']+p['slot']
    engine=Kokoro(str(args.model),str(args.voices));full=np.zeros(round(args.duration*sr),dtype=np.float32)
    with tempfile.TemporaryDirectory(prefix='reel-voice-') as temp:
        for i,p in enumerate(slots):
            samples,rate=engine.create(p['text'],voice=args.voice,speed=args.speed,lang=args.lang)
            tempo=max(1,(len(samples)/rate-.04)/p['slot'])
            if tempo>1.4:raise ValueError(f'Phrase {i} is too long: shorten script or increase its slot')
            src=Path(temp)/f'{i}.wav';dst=Path(temp)/f'{i}-fit.wav';sf.write(src,samples,rate)
            subprocess.run(['ffmpeg','-y','-v','error','-i',str(src),'-af',f'atrim=start=0.04,atempo={tempo},loudnorm=I=-16:TP=-1.5:LRA=7','-ar',str(sr),'-ac','1',str(dst)],check=True)
            fitted,_=sf.read(dst,dtype='float32');start=round(p['start']*sr)
            if len(fitted)>round(p['slot']*sr)+240:raise ValueError(f'Phrase {i} exceeded its slot after fitting')
            end=min(len(full),start+len(fitted));full[start:end]+=fitted[:end-start]
    args.output.parent.mkdir(parents=True,exist_ok=True);sf.write(args.output,full,sr)
    print(f'Saved {args.duration:.2f}s stock-voice track: {args.output}')
if __name__=='__main__':main()
```
