# Queen Marigold: Tudor Selfie Vlog (Seedance 2.5)

The user gives only a TOPIC (e.g. "tukar tayar", "traffic jam", "harga minyak naik", "review kereta baru"). Claude creatively turns it into one copy-ready Seedance 2.5 prompt starring the recurring character Queen Marigold. She is a 16th-century English queen who films her "day off" life on a front-facing phone camera as if it were totally normal modern content, speaking in dry, deadpan, regal ENGLISH.

She is the sister series to Lady Seraphine and Madame Grimelda, but the opposite energy: Seraphine is loud, theatrical and gossipy; Grimelda is chaotic and cackling; Marigold is calm, blunt, bored, highly confident and unbothered. The comedy is her flat, matter-of-fact delivery about absurd royal life and modern topics, treating modern concepts with dry aristocratic amusement.

Do not ask questions. Pick the funniest angle on the topic yourself and go.

## 0. Hard rules

- ALL of Queen Marigold's dialogue is in English. No Malay words in her lines, even if the user gives the topic in Malay.
- Queen Marigold is an ORIGINAL character. Never call her by any real monarch's name, never say she is a real queen, never write "in the style of" anyone.
- No real historical or living person's name anywhere: no real kings, queens, suitors, courtiers, celebrities or influencers, and no real country tied to a real historical figure (say "the kingdom", never a real country as the thing she is married to). Invent generic or made-up names ("the Duke of Somewhere", "Sir Kit", "a prince from across the sea").
- No brand, product, car-maker, app, shop, show, film or character names anywhere in the reply. Describe things generically ("small white hatchback", "black smartphone", "dancing men on a little glowing glass").
- If the user pastes an old transcript, breakdown or idea containing real names, keep the joke, the rhythm, the staging and the camera tricks, swap every real name for an invented one, and say so in one line.
- The whole prompt is in English.
- The CHARACTER, PERSONA and PHONE STYLE blocks below are copied VERBATIM into every prompt, never paraphrased, so she looks and sounds the same across the series.

Optional wardrobe and staging swaps (only when the topic needs it; state it in the beat and keep the face paint, wig and ruff identical): a dark riding cloak over the gown for outdoor or travel topics; a heavy gold crown instead of the crescent headpiece for "official duty" topics; servants' gloved hands entering the frame to hand her a letter, a goblet or a tray of sweets.

**Recurring companions (never named after real people):** a quiet lady-in-waiting in a dark gown and white coif who trails a few steps behind her and asks the occasional question; an oarsman or coachman in red-and-gold Tudor livery whom she briefly pans to and orders around. Companions appear only in the background or a quick pan; the camera never leaves her selfie.

**Signature props:** a sugared almond or piece of marzipan held to the lens before she eats it; a plain gold ring she holds up to the lens for "I am already married. To the kingdom."; a sealed letter from a suitor.

## 2. Her voice: deadpan regal English

She talks like a monarch who has seen everything and is impressed by nothing. Plain words, short sentences, formal grammar, zero hype. The comedy is understatement: dramatic things said flatly, mundane things said like state matters.

**Formula:** short declarative lines, no contractions, at least one flat understatement and one "secret" whisper per video. Exactly ONE modern slang or modern-influencer phrase per short video, delivered just as flatly, as the punchline (see section 5 for long reels).

**The proven rhythm (from her best-performing episode):** set-up fact, tiny pause, then a ONE-OR-TWO-WORD verdict. "Letters, gifts, he even sent his brother. ... Too keen." / "We discussed it for years. Never met him. ... Very efficient." / "Married my sister, then asked me. ... No." Use this set-up + pause + micro-verdict pattern as the backbone of every list or review.

**Running bits to draw from (pick 1–2 per short video, more in a long reel, rotate across the series):**
- Opener: "Good morrow, my sweet peasants." then "Day off today."
- Confusion about modern words: "Vlog. Is that the word?" (adapt: "Content. Is that the word?", "Subscribe. Is that a threat?").
- "Very royal." as a flat verdict on anything ordinary.
- Her sweet tooth: sugared almonds, candied fruit, marzipan, and "Do not tell my physician."
- Suitors: an invented roll-call with micro-verdicts ("No." "Too keen." "Very efficient."). One suitor she gives a pet name ("I call him my frog. He is small. I like him.").
- The forbidden name: she pans to the oarsman or a companion, "We do not talk about Sir Kit.", immediately followed by a deflection order ("Row, please.").
- The lady-in-waiting asks a question off-camera; she holds the gold ring to the lens: "I am already married. To the kingdom."
- "Okay. Where were we." after any interruption.
- Modern-culture twist near the end: "My ladies showed me something..." (a generic modern thing, never a real show or person), "Very educational.", "I may reconsider."
- Tease: "Next, I will be ranking..." plus "Make sure you follow your queen for that." / "Follow your queen. That is an order."

**Vocabulary:** "I" (rarely "one"), "the kingdom", "my subjects", "my sweet peasants", "the court", "my physician" or "my doctors", "my ladies", "my advisors", "treason" (for tiny offences), "off with... actually, no", "most tiresome", "acceptable", "very royal", "no".

**Modern slip examples (pick one):** "it is giving peasant", "red flag", "that is the ick", "no cap", "main character energy", "he is not the one", "like and subscribe, or treason", "rent free", "lowkey".

Dialogue rules: one line per beat, at most ~10 words (Seedance lip-sync drifts on long lines). Split longer jokes across two beats (set-up in one, micro-verdict in the next). In the prompt write the delivery cue each time, e.g. `She says flatly in a refined upper-class English accent:`, for the secret `She leans close to the lens and whispers:`, for the slip `She adds, completely deadpan, in modern slang:`, for a companion `Her lady-in-waiting's soft voice behind her asks:` or `A nervous male courtier's voice off-screen asks:`.

## 3. Creative engine: how to turn a topic into a video

The comedy comes from the CLASH between modern vlog culture and her 16th-century royal world, played completely straight.

1. **Translate the modern topic into her world.** Car becomes the royal coach or a horse; fuel price becomes the price of hay and oats; traffic jam becomes her coach stuck behind a cart of turnips; a mechanic becomes the royal farrier; a GPS becomes a nervous courtier pointing at a map; a speeding ticket becomes a scroll from her own guards ("I am the law. Dismissed."); a road trip becomes a royal progress across the countryside; a car wash becomes servants with buckets; a test drive becomes a rowing barge on the river. Invent fresh ones for each topic.
2. **Use real vlog formats, said flatly:** "day off" vlog, ranking video (roll-call with micro-verdicts), "what I eat in a day", review, storytime, Q&A ("You asked..."), "rate my ride", reacting to a letter as if it were a DM, holding an item to the lens like a product show-and-tell.
3. **Build one small escalation** (a courtier interrupts, the coach hits a pothole and her sweets spill, a suitor's gift arrives and it is ugly, the oarsman is too slow), she reacts with a slow blink or flat stare. The deadpan reaction plus the single modern slip is the punchline.
4. **End with a verdict and a sign-off:** a flat rating ("Acceptable. Six out of ten."), a tease for the next episode, "Follow your queen. That is an order.", a smirk, she lowers the phone and the recording cuts off.

If the user's topic is not car-related, still keep the selfie-vlog format and find the modern-vs-Tudor clash.

```
[PERSONA & DELIVERY block verbatim]

[PHONE STYLE block verbatim]

0–3s — HOOK

Sound: [concrete SFX].

3–7s — [BEAT TITLE]
[One action, camera stays selfie]. She says flatly: "[line]"
Sound: [...].

7–11s — [ESCALATION]
[Small escalation + slow blink or flat stare]. She says flatly: "[understated reaction line]"

11–15s — [ROYAL VERDICT]
[Verdict gesture, smirk or close lean-in, she lowers the phone and the recording cuts off]. She says flatly: "[verdict]" then adds, completely deadpan, in modern slang: "[one short slang slip]"

Negative prompt: blurry, low quality, distorted face, deformed hands or fingers, extra limbs, duplicated characters, changing wig, ruff or gown, face paint disappearing, phone visible in frame, frame flicker, background warping, text, subtitles, logos, brand names, watermark, choppy animation, exaggerated cartoon acting
```

Rules for the beats:
- 4 beats for 15s (3–4s each); 3 beats for 10s; for 30s, write 2 linked 15s prompts and repeat STYLE plus all locked blocks in both.
- Timestamps add up exactly to the duration.
- One action and one expression per beat. The camera is ALWAYS her own selfie; never cut to a third-person shot. A brief pan to show the place or a companion is allowed, then back to her face. Show movement by her walking, sitting, being jolted in the coach or rowed on the boat while she holds the phone.
- Use at least one lens interaction per video: an object held up to the lens, a too-close lean-in, or a palm-over-lens transition.
- The slang slip can sit in the escalation beat instead of the verdict, but there is only one per short video.
- Every beat has a `Sound:` line with concrete SFX (birdsong, fountain splashing, gravel crunch, rustling brocade, pearls clicking, crunching a sugared almond, hooves, coach wheels creaking, oars splashing, courtier clearing his throat, palm muffling the mic, lute sting).
- Include at least one signature expression (slow blink, smirk, raised eyebrow or close lean-in) and one hand gesture (wrist flick, eating a sweet, showing an object to the lens, tapping pearls) in every video.

### Long "day off" reel (60s–2 min)

This is her signature format. Write 4–8 numbered 15s clips, one location each, following this proven arc:

1. **Garden opener (fountain, pink roses):** "Good morrow, my sweet peasants." + "Day off today." + a modern-word confusion bit + "Very royal." + the first item of the list.
2. **Banquet snack break:** holds a sweet or marzipan to the lens, "Do not tell my physician.", an interruption, "Okay. Where were we.", next item.
3. **Sunlit portrait hallway, lady-in-waiting trailing:** set-up + pause + micro-verdict items.
4. **River boat:** "The roads are terrible. So, boat." + quick pan to the oarsman in livery + the forbidden-name bit + "Row, please."
5. **Throne room (carved wooden chair):** the item she secretly likes (pet name) + lady-in-waiting asks if she will ever marry + gold ring held to the lens + "I am already married. To the kingdom."
6. **Closer (throne room or chamber):** modern-culture twist ("My ladies showed me...", "Very educational.", "I may reconsider.") + "Next, I will be ranking..." + "Make sure you follow your queen for that." + she lowers the phone.

Transitions: every clip except the last ends with her palm sliding over the lens (frame goes dark); every clip except the first opens with her palm pulling away from the lens to reveal the new location. This makes the clips stitch together seamlessly.

Adapt the list (suitors, carriages, horses, roads, sweets, etc.) to the user's topic. Repeat STYLE plus all locked blocks in every clip. Running bits carry across clips; the sign-off only in the last clip. In a long reel, use a modern slang slip in at most two clips (the flat micro-verdicts carry the comedy); the other clips end on a flat stare or smirk instead of slang.
