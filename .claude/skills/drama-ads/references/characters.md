# Characters

A character is anything in the story that acts and wants: a human, an animal, a creature, a machine, an object, food. Each cast member has `kind`, `human` or `other` (`script-format.md`). Every character below holds for all of them; Human characters applies only to `kind: human`, Other characters only to `kind: other`. Every character is built the way the film's `style` builds its characters (`styles.md`): the facts say what the character is, the style how it is drawn.

## Every character

Design every character so the viewer reads it in ten seconds for its role and the story's genre: its shape, size, colours, wardrobe and bearing say at once who it is and how to feel about it. Appearance and wardrobe proposals make that read strong, and say shapes, bodies and clothes in plain words.

- Characters who share scenes differ at a glance: a different silhouette or size and a different leading colour (for humans, also a different hair shade or cut). Relatives share one feature, named the same way. A double, a twin or one character in two forms is the exception: the same face or design (`sheet-realism.md` for a human in live action, `sheet-stylized.md` for every other character), told apart by one visible detail the story gives (the outfit, a mark).
- A character in a disguise the story reveals: its sheet shows the disguise, and the reveal is an effect it owns (`plan-and-effects.md`). A thing a character wears that passes to another is, in 3D and 2D, a prop with its own sheet, left off the wearer's sheet, or the shot shows two.
- Each character carries one signature, a colour, a mark or an accessory that fits its part, written in its wardrobe facts, or in its appearance facts when it wears nothing, and kept in every scene.
- `voice` says how it sounds, its timbre and register, fitting its size and its part, with no pace words.
- NO SENSITIVE CONTENT (`story-rules.md`) covers every character: nothing sexualised; human and humanlike characters are always clothed; a non-human character in its natural form (fur, peel, casing) is not undressed and needs no clothes.
- No children: no child or baby is ever on screen, as a character, an extra or a prop; no human under 18, and no character of any kind presented as a child or baby, or as the young of its kind, in its facts, its name or its role. A story that needs one keeps it off screen (a closed door, a sound, the others' reactions).

## Human characters (`kind: human`)

These rules hold for a human in every style, a cartoon human included.
An age fact gives one number of 18 or more, never a range or a word such as adult or mature: every human character is 18 or older. `gender` is woman or man.
A human character the story presents as attractive, of any gender and in any role, is proposed as strikingly attractive for the setting: a striking face, healthy skin and well-kept hair, with the age, build, makeup and clothes their role, period and place give them. There is no default body type, age, model look or glam makeup: an attractive character still dresses and stands as their job and world make them. Every other human is cast for the part, plain or striking as the story needs. A specific look the script gives is kept exactly in the facts, within NO SENSITIVE CONTENT (`story-rules.md`), never covered or toned down.
A spicier look is the user's option for human characters only, never a default in any genre: only when the user asks for one (medium or high in the sheet texts) do the facts carry it, because the sheet never changes build or wardrobe, and never for a character the story frames as a school student. It is a closer, shorter or more fitted cut of the same period garments, a lower neckline, heels for a woman where the setting allows them, for a man a fitted shirt with the top two or three buttons undone below the collarbones, and at high garments skin-tight or split with a deep plunging neckline for a woman and a powerfully muscular build for a man; always within NO SENSITIVE CONTENT.
For every human character with an image:

- Every human's appearance facts state their ethnicity and skin tone. The script decides first. Then a setting clearly of one people, or a name that clearly belongs to one heritage, decides it; a name common across many heritages decides nothing. Otherwise the film's human cast is mixed: its unrelated human characters come from a mix of ethnicities, as a film made for a broad international audience casts them, never one default for everyone. Blood relatives share one. An unstated ethnicity changes from image to image.
- Hair is proposed with an exact named shade and a cut that fits the period and the part.
- The wardrobe starts from the job: what that person really wears for it in the story's place, period and genre, readable at a glance. Status shows in the fabric and the finish.
- Each garment is its own piece with its fabric and colour, never "layered".
- The wardrobe fact is the outfit the character wears through the film (`script-format.md`), each garment with its fabric, colour and fit. Where a scene's occasion or a time jump makes it wrong, the character wears another outfit there, a form with its own sheet made in step 3 (`forms`, `script-format.md`; tested: made from the master with only the outfit changed, the sheet kept the face): kept through every scene, one outfit stayed on two leads in scenes whose occasion it did not fit.
  Accessories fit the role, the period and the genre, never a default set for every human: what this person really wears or carries for their part, and none where the part has none. Wealth shows in fine fabrics and sharp tailoring; everyday clothes fit the way that person really wears them, with a clear silhouette.

## Other characters (`kind: other`)

Anything that is not human, a hybrid of two things included. Its role reads from its shape, colours and bearing.

- `form` says what it is in a few plain words, the noun the image model draws. Never an existing character, mascot or brand (`styles.md`).
- `appearance` gives, in plain literal words: its shape; its size against the others; its material and texture; its colours; the face it has (which features and where they sit) or that it has none and what the viewer reads instead (a light, a sound, a posture); and how it moves. Only what stays the same in every scene.
- An animal from the user's photos acts as an animal and stays silent unless the user asks for it to talk. A talking animal is then the style's normal in a cartoon, and the film's one world rule in live action. A brief that says the characters talk does not make the animal talk: in three test films such a brief gave the animal lines the user did not want.
- `wardrobe` holds only what it wears or carries, each piece with its material and colour, or "none".
- `age` and `gender` appear only when the story gives them; an age is a number of 18 or more. It is never a child or baby, nor the young of its kind.
- A character with a humanlike body is always clothed, and its clothes follow the human wardrobe rules above.
